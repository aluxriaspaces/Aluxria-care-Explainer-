1. Introduction

   The Opportunity: A New Chapter for Corowa

   Federation Council is proud to invite Expressions of Interest (EOIs) from visionary, high calibre
   operators for the management and strategic redevelopment of the Ball Park Caravan Park.

   Located on the pristine foreshore of the iconic Murray River in Corowa, this site represents one of the
   region’s most significant tourism assets, now primed for a transformation into a premier Australian
   destination.

   This is more than a long lease; it is a unique opportunity to collaborate with Council in shaping a
   contemporary, sustainable, and commercially viable holiday park.

   Council seeks an innovative partner with the proven capability to unlock the site’s potential and deliver
   contemporary infrastructure that reflects the evolving expectations of the modern traveler.


   Why Ball Park?

   Ball Park Caravan Park represents an exceptional opportunity to serve as a cornerstone of regional
   tourism, anchored by its direct, picturesque frontage onto a premier and highly accessible section of
   the iconic Murray River. Its strategic location offers a unique blend of accessibility and leisure, sitting
   just a comfortable 3-hour and 15-minute drive from the Melbourne metropolitan market.

   Step into the heart of Corowa, a town where significant Australian history meets a vibrant modern
   lifestyle. Known as the “Birthplace of Federation,” Corowa offers a rich tapestry of heritage trails and
   grand architecture that now houses award-winning whisky distilleries and artisan chocolate makers.
   Beyond the town center the surrounding region opens up into a gourmet goldmine; here, century-old
   vineyards and farm-gate stalls invite guests to taste the very best of the harvest.

   For the active traveler, Ball Park is perfectly positioned within a high-value recreation precinct. Guests
   can spend their days cycling along scenic trails that connect historic villages or take to the water for a
   peaceful paddle by the iconic river red gums. With world-class fishing spots, bird filled wetlands, and
   plenty of room to roam, the region provides the perfect backdrop for outdoor adventure. Corowa’s
   proximity to the renowned Indigo and King Valley wine regions and its location less than two hours
   from world-class ski fields make Ball Park an ideal year-round destination for a diverse range of
   travellers.

   The activation of this park is a primary economic driver for Corowa and the wider Federation region.
   Following strategic remediation, the park currently operates on a reduced footprint, leaving a
   significant portion including highly valued foreshore areas as a blank canvas for development. Both
   the community and the Council share a high ambition to elevate the park’s profile, enrich the visitor
   journey, and contribute meaningfully to the long-term economic and social vitality of the region.

2. A Secure, Long-Term Partnership

   This EOI is conducted under the Local Government Act 1993 (NSW) and the Crown Land
   Management Act 2016. Council is offering a secure foundation for investment with the following tenure
   options:

   21-Year Initial Lease: A robust term to support significant infrastructure delivery.

   30-Year Potential: Council is actively pursuing Ministerial approval for an extended 30-year lease to
   provide further commercial certainty. Council is currently undertaking the necessary Plan of
   Management requirements and agency consultations to facilitate this.

   Proponent Requirement: Proponents must clearly indicate within their proposal whether they are
   seeking the standard 21-year lease, a 30-year lease, or if their proposal is contingent only upon the
   longer 30-year period.

   IMPORTANT NOTICE: Please note that the potential 30-year tenure is subject to formal Ministerial
   approval and is not guaranteed at this stage. Proponents should structure their capital investment
   plans and business models to remain viable based on the 21-year term, treating the 30-year extension
   as a strategic opportunity.

4. Site Overview and Context

    Regional Context

    Corowa is a substantial and historically significant river town that serves as a key service centre for
    the productive agricultural lands flanking the Murray River. The district supports a diverse mix of
    farming activities, including broad acre and irrigated cropping, livestock, and significant value adding
    agricultural businesses including dairy, pork, pet food and cereal production. These are
    complemented by a range of supporting industries.
    The region is also a prominent tourism destination, offering recreational activities on the Murray
    River and Lake Mulwala, with convenient access to internationally recognised wineries and the snow
    fields.

Management History and Strategic Remediation

Following the conclusion of previous long-term lease arrangements, Council assumed direct management
of the park. Between 2019 and 2020, Council undertook a significant remediation program to resolve
legacy compliance risks including electrical, waste-water, and fire safety, resulting in the removal of
approximately 180 non-compliant annual vans and cabins.

The Park now operates on a reduced, fully compliant footprint via a labour-management model. While this
has provided essential accommodation in the interim, Council has successfully navigated the challenges
of the remediation, the 2022 floods, and the pandemic to prepare the site as a "blank canvas" ready for
professional redevelopment.


Strategic Context and Precinct Investment

In October 2020, Council adopted a comprehensive Masterplan which identifies the "Pink Shaded" area
as Stage 1 of the redevelopment (refer to overflow area in figure 2 above). This area is designated for
high-class, elevated short-stay cabins designed to capitalise on extensive Murray River views.

The future development of the park including any proposal to formalise the full‑time use of the overflow
area, will be the sole responsibility of the incoming lessee. The incoming lessee will be entirely responsible

The commercial appeal of the park is further enhanced by over $15 million in recent infrastructure

•      The $12 million year-round Corowa Aquatic Centre.
•      Over 5km of concrete pathways and regional walking trails.
•      Upgraded river access roads, parking, and extensive landscaping.
•      Restoration of historic assets, including a red brick well and new public shelter areas.

5. EOI Strategic Objectives

Council seeks to engage a capable operator who can:

   •   Deliver a high-quality, compliant, and commercially viable park operation that meets contemporary
       visitor expectations and regulatory obligations.
   •   Identify and pursue redevelopment opportunities that unlock the potential of the site and support
       long-term strategic growth.
   •   Maintain the current park operations whilst developing the currently vacant unapproved section/s
       to continue to provide an important accommodation option.
   •   Enhance accommodation offerings and increase visitation, with a focus on diversified product mix,
       seasonal activation, and market responsiveness.
   •   Prepare a comprehensive development plan outlining proposed capital investment, staging, and
       alignment with planning and environmental (regulatory) frameworks.
   •   Elevate the visitor experience and tourism value through improved amenities, service standards,
       and integration with regional attractions.
   •   Ensure ongoing compliance with relevant environmental, planning and Crown land management
       requirements, including obligations under the Local Government Act and other relevant legislation.
   •   Conside implementation of innovative, eco-friendly initiatives—such as solar energy integration,
       advanced water recycling systems, and robust waste reduction strategies

6. Procurement Process

Federation Council has decided to utilise the selective tender method pursuant to section 166(b) of the
Local Government (General) Regulations. Council will conduct the procurement process using E Procure
via https://www.federationcouncil.nsw.gov.au/Council/Tendering-Supply in three progressive stages:

Stage 1 -
Expression of Interest (EOI):

Initial submissions are invited from suitably qualified and experienced operators outlining their capability,
vision, and alignment with Council’s strategic objectives for the Ball Park Caravan Park.

Stage 2 -
Selective Tender - Request for Detailed Proposals (RDP):

All proponents from Stage 1 will be assessed and following a Council determination via a confidential
Council meeting, Council will invite the final selected proponent/s to submit comprehensive proposals
detailing their operational approach, development concepts, financial models, and compliance strategies.

*Further detailed criteria will be provided at this time to the selected proponents*.

Stage 3 -
Negotiation:

Council will then assess the final tender/s submitted, and via a Council determination resolve to accept a
tender or refuse tender/s submitted.

Council has various means under the Local Government Act at this time to proceed further depending on
the outcomes of this process, including entering into negotiations with one or more shortlisted proponents
to refine proposals, clarify commitments, and establish a pathway to a partnership.

Council reserves the right to proceed to Stages 2 and 3 with one or more selected proponents at its
discretion.

7. Scope of Proposal - Stage 1: Expression of Interest (EOI)
Proponents are required to address the following five criteria in their EOI submission:

In this initial stage, Proponents are required to provide a high-level conceptual overview and demonstrate
their ability to deliver a premier tourism asset. Submissions should focus on the following:

1. Capacity and Previous Experience

   •   Provide an overview of the proponent’s history in the caravan park, hospitality, or tourism sector.
   •   Demonstrate a proven track record of managing similar-scale assets or successful delivery of
       caravan park re-development projects.
   •   Provide references or case studies of current or past operations that align with the strategic
       objectives for Ball Park.

2. Vision and Accommodation Concept

   •   Outline the high-level vision for the park’s transformation.
   •   Propose an indicative accommodation mix (e.g., glamping, cabins, campsites) that aligns with
       modern tourism trends and market demand.
   •   Identify the target visitor segments the proponent intends to attract.

3. Preliminary Development Approach

   •   Present a conceptual "look and feel" for the park’s future state.
   •   Provide high-level thoughts on staging and how the park can transition from its current footprint to
       a fully activated precinct.

4. Indicative Guest Experience

   •   Outline the philosophy behind the proposed guest services and communal facilities.
   •   Identify potential commercial activities (e.g., café, equipment hire) that could enhance the visitor
       journey / experience.

5. High-Level Financial and Term Preferences

   •   Specify the preferred initial lease term and any high-level requirements for the commercial model
       to ensure project viability.

9. Stage 2: Request for Detailed Proposals (RFDP) Scope
*Only Shortlisted Proponents will be invited to submit a detailed, technical, and financially binding
proposal addressing the following criteria*

1. Detailed Capacity and Financial Position

   •   Provide detailed resumes of the key management team and operational staff.
   •   Demonstrate financial capacity to fund the proposed capital works, including bank references or
       proof of funding.

2. Detailed Accommodation Strategy

   •   Finalise the proposed accommodation types, providing specific numbers for powered/unpowered
       sites, cabins (including disability accessible units), glamping, and annual agreement offerings.
   •   Provide a definitive timeline and staging plan for the delivery of all accommodation units.

3. Guest Services and Operational Management

   •   Detail the full suite of guest services, amenities, and recreational offerings.
   •   Provide a comprehensive plan covering: reception operations, communal facility maintenance, and
       digital service platforms.
   •   Describe the specific strategies to increase occupancy and improve the visitor experience year-
       round.

4. Development and Capital Investment Plan

   •   Present a comprehensive concept development plan and site masterplan.
   •   Provide detailed capital investment estimates, broken down by stage and infrastructure type.
   •   Outline the proposed tariffs, pricing strategy, and guest value proposition.
   •   Demonstrate full alignment with planning controls, environmental obligations, and Crown Land
       management requirements.

5. Binding Financial Offer

   •   State the proposed base annual rent (exclusive of GST) for both Crown and Council owned
       components of land and the preferred review schedule (CPI or fixed %).
   •   Clarify the ownership structure of assets and provide a clear "exit strategy" for the transfer or
       removal of assets at the end of the lease.
   •   Commit to a specific maintenance fund percentage for the upkeep of communal infrastructure.

6. Strategic Marketing and Regional Integration

   •   Provide a detailed marketing and branding strategy, including digital engagement plans.

   •   Outline specific proposed partnerships with regional tourism (e.g., North of the Murray)
   •   Include a calendar of seasonal activation, event hosting, and community engagement strategies.

7. Compliance and Risk Management

   •   Provide a detailed risk management plan for park operations and capital works.
   •   Demonstrate how all commercial activities will be managed to ensure safety, compliance, and
